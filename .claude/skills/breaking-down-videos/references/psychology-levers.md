# Psychology levers

The names section 5 of a breakdown uses. Name a lever only when you can point at the line or
frame that pulls it, with its timestamp. A label with no evidence behind it doesn't go in.

A short-form video has three jobs, in order: stop the scroll (first 3 seconds), hold attention
to the end, and get one action at the end. Most levers serve one of the three.

## Stop the scroll

**Pattern Interrupt.** Something the feed hasn't trained the viewer to skip: an odd first
frame, a sudden movement, a sound, a claim that doesn't match the setting. Evidence is the
first frame or first word.

**Curiosity Gap.** The opening tells the viewer something exists and withholds what it is.
Name where the gap opens and the second it closes. A gap that never closes is a broken promise.

**Loss Framing.** The hook is about what the viewer is losing or getting wrong, not what they
could gain. "You're wasting…" beats "You could save…" because a loss is felt more sharply.

**Identity Callout.** The first line names who this is for, tightly enough that the right
viewer feels picked out: "If you run a med spa…", "Every first-time founder…".

## Hold attention

**Open Loop.** A question or setup the video promises to answer later, so leaving early
means missing the answer. Quote the line that opens it and the line that closes it.

**Re-hook.** A line mid-video that resets attention just as it would drop: "But here's the
part nobody tells you". Count them and quote each one.

**Variable Reward.** The viewer can't predict when the next payoff lands: uneven beat lengths,
a list where each item is better than the last, a reveal that arrives early or late.

**Cognitive Ease.** Short words, one idea per line, on-screen text that matches the voice,
visuals that show what's said. The brain keeps watching what costs nothing to follow.

**Vulnerability.** The creator admits a mistake, a fear or a loss. It buys trust, and trust
buys watch time. Quote the admission.

**Proof.** Numbers, screenshots, results, a before and after, a name the viewer trusts. Note
whether it's shown on screen or only said.

## Convert

**Identity Alignment.** Acting on the video (saving, following, commenting) says something
the viewer wants to be true about themselves.

**Reciprocity.** The video gives so much away that the ask feels like a fair trade.

**Single Ask.** One action at the end, stated once, clearly. Two asks split the response.

**Consistency.** A small yes earlier ("you've felt this, right?") makes the bigger yes at the
end feel natural.

---

When a video shows a principle none of these cover, describe it plainly in a sentence, with
its evidence, and name it in your own words. Don't force the wrong label.
